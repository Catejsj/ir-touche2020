[1] SOURCE
    collection   Webis-Touche-2020, Task 1 (argument retrieval)
    shared task  Touche @ CLEF 2020 (Bondarenko et al.)
    distributed  as part of BEIR (Thakur et al., NeurIPS 2021)
    obtained     huggingface.co/datasets/BeIR/webis-touche2020
    documents    args.me corpus - arguments crawled from five debate
                 portals (debatewise, idebate, debatepedia, debate.org,
                 canadian parliament)
    licence      CC BY-SA 4.0

    The retrieval task: given a controversial question a person might
    actually type - 'Should teachers get tenure?' - return arguments that
    help them take a side. Relevance is argument quality and stance
    support, not topical aboutness, which is why the judges used a scale
    rather than a yes/no.

[2] CLEANING
    documents as distributed   382,545

    Unicode is normalised (NFKC) and whitespace collapsed, per field.
    Nothing is lowercased or stemmed here - that belongs to the indexing
    stage, and the raw text has to survive so results can be shown to a
    reader in the form the debater wrote them.

[2b] POOLED SUBSET
    The full collection is 369,390 documents and about 110
    million words. Building an index over it exhausted the memory on
    the machine available to us - the process was killed by the
    kernel. Rather than hide that, we retrieve over a pooled subset,
    which is standard practice when a full collection is out of reach:

      every JUDGED document kept      2,095
      random unjudged distractors     57,905 (seed 42)
      collection searched             60,000 (16.2% of the full corpus)

    (2,095 distinct documents carrying 2,210 judgments - a few documents
    were judged for more than one query.)

    Why this keeps the evaluation valid: a metric can only be computed
    over documents a human judged, and every one of them is still
    here. No query loses a relevant document, and no measure changes
    definition.

    What it does change, and we say so on the slide: the task is
    easier. A system now sifts 60,000 documents instead of 369,390,
    so there are roughly six times fewer chances to rank an irrelevant
    document above a relevant one. Our scores are therefore an
    upper bound on what the same systems would achieve on the full
    collection, and they are not comparable to published Touche
    numbers. Re-running at full scale is the first item in future
    work - the code takes --max-docs 0 and needs only more memory.

[4] RELEVANCE JUDGMENTS  (this is the part that decides the evaluation)
     level   judgments    share  meaning
         0       1,278   57.8%  judged NOT relevant
         1         296   13.4%  relevant
         2         636   28.8%  highly relevant

    documents judged per query   mean 45.1, min 40, max 52
    RELEVANT (level >= 1) per query  mean 19.0, min 6, max 32

    Two consequences, both of which shape the evaluation:

    1. Graded relevance. 636 documents are rated 2 and
       296 are rated 1. A system that puts level-2
       arguments on top is better than one that puts level-1 arguments
       on top, and nDCG is the measure that can see that difference.
       Precision/recall/F1 cannot - they need a yes/no, so we state the
       threshold we use (level >= 1 counts as relevant) rather than
       leaving it implicit.

    2. 1,278 documents were judged and found NOT relevant.
       Most collections omit these, so a wrong answer is indistinguishable
       from an unjudged one. Here, when a system ranks one of these in the
       top 10, a human has explicitly said it does not answer the query -
       which is a much stronger error analysis than 'not in our list'.

    Unjudged documents: the other 382,000-odd documents were never shown
    to a judge. Standard TREC practice treats them as not relevant, and
    that is what we do, but it is an assumption and it is in the
    limitations.

[5] LEGAL AND ETHICAL NOTES
    - Licence: CC BY-SA 4.0. Reuse, modification and redistribution are
      permitted with attribution and share-alike. The Touche task and the
      BEIR paper are both cited in the references.
    - Provenance: args.me crawled five public debate portals. We use the
      published research corpus, not our own crawl, so no site's terms of
      service are engaged by this project.
    - Personal data: arguments were posted publicly under usernames.
      The BEIR distribution carries no author field, no username and no
      timestamp - only an id, a title and the argument text. There is
      therefore nothing to anonymise, and we add nothing back.
    - We do not attempt to identify authors, link arguments to people, or
      aggregate a person's positions across documents.
    - Content: the corpus is debate material on abortion, gun control,
      religion, immigration and similar. Some arguments are offensive.
      They are retrieved and scored, never endorsed; any example shown in
      the report is chosen to illustrate a retrieval behaviour, and
      slides carry a content note.
    - Our system ranks arguments by how well they match a query. It does
      not judge whether an argument is true, and it should not be
      presented as doing so.
